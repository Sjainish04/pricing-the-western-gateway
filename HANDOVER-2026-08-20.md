## 2. Findings, in order of how much they should change what you say

### 2.1 The study's second headline finding is fragile

The rejoin fraction was described as the driver of the diversion result while
being **hard-coded in `routes.csv`** — so it was the one parameter the tornado
and Monte Carlo could not reach. It is now a `Config` field, swept by both.
The default is bit-identical to the old value, pinned by a test.

Sweeping it 0.20 → 0.90:

| Conclusion | Robust? |
|---|---|
| \$10 screenline recommendation — diversion, revenue, equity, feasibility | Yes, across the whole range |
| \$5 screenline turns diversion negative | Yes |
| **\$4 freeway-only breaches the 10% local-street cap** | **No — flips at 0.49** |

The assumed value is 0.55. **The finding survives on a margin of 0.06 in a
number nothing local has ever measured.** Keep making the claim — but attach
the dependence, and quote `breakeven_rejoin_fraction()` rather than implying
the leak is certain. The recommendation itself does not depend on it, which is
the more important half.

### 2.2 The corridor is not the New Jersey case

The previous handover said this model "has the same structure" as FHWA's NYC
finding (NJ residents paying 45% of tolls on 24% of trips, ratio 1.88). **It
does not.** Under the recommended design the 905 pays **36.2%** of the charge
on **40.0%** of trips — ratio **0.904** — and receives **44.4%** of the recycled
benefit, making it a net recipient by 8 points. Lakeshore West GO gives
west-GTA travellers a way out and enough take it. Holds under a freeway-only
design too (0.986).

The burden lands on **inner Etobicoke** instead: Alderwood at **0.81%** of
household income, paying 1.33× its share of trips. If the submission claims a
905-pays-disproportionately story, it is wrong and a judge with the FHWA paper
open will catch it.

**54%** of geographic burden variation is not explained by income, which is
what justifies the separate page rather than folding it into §7.

### 2.3 Sequencing is worth more than the price

At an identical \$10 charge, expected discounted welfare over ten years:

| Path | Precedent | Support at gate | P(enact) | E[disc W] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transit, pilot, then referendum | Stockholm | 73% | 0.96 | **0.429** |
| Transit first, then charge | Stockholm, no pilot | 52% | 0.56 | 0.168 |
| Transit, ride-hail surcharge, cordon | NYC 2019–25 | 52% | 0.56 | 0.118 |
| Charge immediately | NYC 2008 | 38% | 0.16 | −0.131 |
| Operations, never charge | Toronto CMP | — | 1.00 | −0.197 |

**0.56 welfare points** separate the best ordering from charging now, at the
same price. None of it is a better policy; all of it is a better chance the
policy exists. The **pilot** does most of the work — it replaces a forecast
with an experience.

Timing frontier: **interior optimum at year 1**, when the transit uplift is
fully delivered. Charging a year earlier costs 0.145 to enactment risk; waiting
to year 6 costs 0.246 to discounting. *Charge as soon as the alternative lands,
not a year later.*

### 2.4 What the 2027 transfer actually does

Not what it looks like. The repair liability moving City → Province **cancels
out of the bargain**: a lump sum shifts a player's deal and no-deal payoffs
equally, so it moves welfare levels but not the incentive to agree. An earlier
version of the module put the transfer's whole effect in that term, where it
silently did nothing; a test now pins it.

What changes is the **Province's fallback**. Today neither side can charge the
Gardiner alone — City owns it, Province authorises — so they hold a mutual
veto. After fall 2027 the Province owns the road *and* authorises itself. The
City's fallback does not improve.

**The option the Province gains is a freeway-only charge** — the design this
study finds pushes +11.6% onto The Queensway. Those are City streets, and the
City would have no standing to object.

The measured loss of leverage is small (<1% of surplus) for a reason worth
saying out loud: that threat is *financially marginal* (finding #4 — fixed
collection costs don't scale). **A threat that loses money is a weak threat.**
The City's exposure is to the diversion, not to the Province's revenue.

The political-cost rate is unobservable, so it is inverted rather than tuned:
the zone of agreement closes at **\$3.91** per charged trip pre-transfer,
**\$8.75** post. The Province *did* refuse in 2017, so its valuation sits above
the pre-transfer threshold — a revealed bound from a real decision.

## 3. Bugs found and fixed — continuing §9 of the last handover

The pattern holds exactly: **every one produced a plausible number, not a
crash.**

15. **Absorption shares above 1.** The flow accounting reported the bypass
    arterial absorbing 3.8× what the expressway lost on capacity assumptions
    where the accounting cannot close. Reads like a finding; is an arithmetic
    impossibility. Those combinations are now rejected *with a reason*.
16. **A missing finance key silently zeroed the bargaining model's central
    term.** `charged_trips_peak` does not exist (it is `charged_vehicles_annual`),
    and `.get(..., 0.0)` meant the political-cost asymmetry — the entire
    mechanism — evaluated to zero while the module returned a complete,
    confident, wrong bargain. Now indexed, so it raises. *The near-miss: the
    key that does exist is already annual, so the original code would also have
    double-annualised it.*
17. **Interim provincial support annualised over the wrong period**, making the
    Province's position look worse the closer the transfer got — backwards.
18. **An ill-conditioned inversion presented as a point estimate.**
    `lakeshore_east` carries ~1 minute of queueing delay at base, so the two
    defensible baselines disagree by 47% in the answer from 6% in the input.
    Now reported as a band with a `conditioning()` diagnostic.

### The performance regression worth remembering

Adding `rejoin_fraction` to the Monte Carlo made **every draw a cache miss** in
`route_composition`, putting pandas `iterrows()` back in the solver's hot loop.
**The suite went from 30s to 3h58m.** The CSV is now parsed once into a
template and the miss path is dict arithmetic: 410µs → 4µs, and the suite runs
faster than before despite 56 new tests.

*This is the same bug as §9-8 in the last handover, re-entering through a new
door.* Any parameter added to a sweep that is read inside the equilibrium loop
will do this again. Check the suite's wall-clock after adding one.

## 3a. Second pass — the items §4 originally left open

All of them are now done. `joint_game.py` is new; `bargaining.py` gains side
payments; `docs/DATA-REQUESTS.md` is new.

### The two games are now one

`joint_game.py`. A charge needs a public that will tolerate it **and** two
governments that can agree, so `P(enacted) = P(voters) × P(deal)`, the second
logistic in the weaker party's bargained surplus. Fall 2027 is ~1.15 years out,
so on an annual grid years 0 and 1 begin before the transfer and year 2 does
not — the phased game's year-1 recommendation sits exactly on the boundary.

At the assumed political cost the deal gate does **not** bind (P ≈ 0.99) and the
joint answer is the phased one. That is the honest baseline. But the Province
refused in 2017, and §2.4's revealed bound puts the true rate above \$3.91.
Sweeping it:

| \$/charged trip | P(deal) pre | P(deal) post | Optimal start | Before transfer? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.90 *(assumed)* | 1.00 | 1.00 | year 1 | yes |
| **3.50** | **0.13** | **0.93** | **year 2** | **no** |
| 8.00 | 0.00 | 0.08 | year 2 | no |
| 10.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | — | no charge at all |

**The recommendation reverses at \$3.50, just below the \$3.91 the 2017 refusal
implies.** On the model's own revealed bound the advice is to *wait* until after
the transfer, not rush a deal before it — the opposite of the leverage story in
§2.4. The mechanism: a zone of agreement survives to a *higher* political cost
post-transfer, because the Province's unilateral fallback carries political cost
of its own, so it is exposed either way. Both arguments are reported; which
dominates is empirical and this study cannot settle it.

### Side payments

The 2017 settlement moved ~\$170M/yr of gas-tax revenue — **30.6× the
corridor's entire net revenue**, and in the opposite direction to anything a
revenue split can express. With transfers available the problem is
transferable-utility: **total** surplus decides whether a deal exists, the split
only decides who banks it. So arguing over corridor revenue is arguing over the
wrong number. `side_payment_curve` separates "hard to agree on" (a transfer
closes it) from "not worth doing" (total surplus negative, nothing closes it).

### Slow tests, properly

The handover's §11D-13 premise was wrong: `test_model.py` is 12s and only two
tests exceed 1s. The cost is in the new game-theory modules, and the worst
single test (92s) was unmarked. 19 tests now marked:

```
pytest -q -m "not slow"    93 passed in 7.8s
pytest -q                 125 passed in 2:55
```

### Distributional map layer

`Map2D` gains a `subareaBurden` layer — circle **area** ∝ trip share (not
radius, which would overstate the largest group by the square of its share),
colour ∝ burden, dashed orange outline for the sub-area outside the City.
`data/subarea_points.json` follows `link_geometry.json` exactly: approximate,
presentation-only, read by nothing in the model, and **points not polygons** —
inventing boundaries would imply a precision the synthetic OD pattern lacks.

### Data requests

`docs/DATA-REQUESTS.md`. Four asks with exact specifications, why each matters,
which published result changes if the answer differs, a covering note, and a
tracking table. §11A-1/A-2 cannot be closed by modelling; this is the nearest
thing to closing them.

## 3b. Deployed

**<https://uttan-congestion.vercel.app>** — static SPA plus one Python
serverless function; `api/index.py` re-exports the same FastAPI app.

**16 pages** now, including three new ones for the game-theory modules that
previously had no interface: **Sequencing** (`/sequencing`), **City ↔
Province** (`/governance`) and **The 2027 deadline** (`/timing`), under a
"When, and who agrees" nav group.

Two things had to be solved first.

*Timeouts.* Bargaining is ~100 s and the joint game ~70 s against a ceiling of
60 s on Vercel Pro and 10 s free — all three best findings would have been
gateway timeouts. Since the API is a pure function of its inputs, they are
generated by `scripts/precompute.py`, committed to
`backend/data/precomputed/`, and served from disk. In production: 837 ms,
435 ms, 891 ms. Guards that make this a cache rather than a second set of
results: a hash of the **full** config stored per file and checked on read
(mismatch ⇒ compute, never serve stale), a test that **recomputes and
compares** — the only check that catches a changed *solver* rather than a
changed *parameter* — and `_source: precomputed|computed` on every response,
with `/api/meta` reporting validity.

*Bundle.* `scipy` was a dependency for the project's whole life, imported
nowhere, and was 123 MB of a 239 MB bundle against a 250 MB limit. Now 116 MB.

**Vercel gotchas worth knowing.** The CLI treats a `requirements.txt` beside a
FastAPI app as a separate deployable *service*: it detected `frontend [Vite]` +
`backend [FastAPI]` and then refused every top-level `buildCommand` /
`functions` / `outputDirectory` with *"the owning service is ambiguous"*,
failing before the build. `version: 2` does not suppress it — the detection is
directory-based. The fix is renaming to `backend/requirements-local.txt`; the
function installs `api/requirements.txt`. The `services` schema would also
work but is marked `private` in `openapi.vercel.sh`, so not something to rest a
submission on. Separately, preview URLs sit behind deployment protection and
302 to SSO, so only production is meaningfully testable.

**Redeploy checklist.** Regenerate the cache after any change to `config.py` or
to the four cached modules, or the endpoints fall back to computing and time
out:

```bash
cd backend && python scripts/precompute.py
python -m pytest tests/test_precomputed.py -q
cd .. && vercel --prod
```

## 3c. Comparative study, and more equity

**New pages:** *Other cities* (`/compare`). **New module:** `comparators.py`.
**New data:** `city_schemes.csv` (nine schemes, all with provenance).

### The comparison is used as a test, not a table

**New York is the out-of-sample check** — it began charging January 2025, the
only North American scheme. It charges \$9 and observed **−11%** entries in
year one. This model at \$9 predicts **−13.1%** AM-peak vehicles: *larger*,
which is the wrong direction for a corridor with weaker alternatives than a
Manhattan cordon.

The leading explanation is that they are not the same quantity — New York's is
year-round all-day zone entries, this is AM-peak screenline, and a peak-only
response is normally the larger. That plausibly accounts for the 2.1 pp gap and
**it is not established that it does**, so the verdict is `unresolved`, not a
passed test and not a refuted model. The MTA publishes CRZ entries by hour, so
this is settleable — **and if the gap survives a like-for-like comparison, the
logit scale is too responsive and this study's demand response is too strong.**
That is the highest-value open item on this page.

### Three places the record contradicts this study

| This study | The record | Why the model cannot see it |
|---|---|---|
| Removes 39.4% of peak delay | **London's traffic reduction was permanent; its *delay* reduction was not** — congestion returned to pre-charge levels within five years | Static equilibrium: no demand growth, no reallocation of freed road space, no induced demand. **The delay headline is a first-year effect, not a steady state** |
| Acceptability from net position + citizen view | Edinburgh lost **74.4–25.6 on trust** — one in four believed the revenue would reach transport | There is no trust term. Toronto's own 2016–17 episode says take it seriously |
| A revenue package helps acceptability | Manchester attached **£3bn** and still lost | Recycling enters through net position, so a big enough package always helps |

And one corroboration: **Stockholm has no income discount of any kind and is
still not regressive**, because wealthy inner-city residents pay most — the same
mechanism as §2.2's finding that the 905 pays less than its share.

### Filling the table was the smaller half

All six operating schemes now carry a sourced charge and traffic response. Two
columns had to be added because the numbers are **not the same quantity**:

- `charge_basis` — London/Milan/New York charge once per **day**;
  Singapore/Stockholm/Gothenburg charge every **passage**. Plotted as two
  series; nothing fitted.
- `traffic_metric` — Milan's usual headline is a 28% *congestion* reduction;
  the figure used is the **31.1%** fall in cordon **entries**, which has counts
  behind it.

**The finding that fell out: price does not order the response.** In the daily
group the cheapest scheme produced the largest reduction and the dearest less
than half — Milan \$5.40 → −31%, London \$19 → −16%. Geometry and alternatives
beat the rate, which is what the model found internally (coverage moved
diversion far more than the rate did).

### Equity: the curve behind the index

`equity.suits_curve` returns the concentration curve the Suits index is
integrated from, so *where* a charge is regressive can be seen. On the
recommended design the widest gap is at **43% of cumulative income paying 57%
of the charge** — the lower-middle, not the very bottom, which the credits reach
first. **A design tuned only on Q1 would leave that untouched and still report
an improved index.**

### A caption that stopped matching its figure

Worth recording as method. I wrote prose saying the price ordering "failed in
both bases"; the data shows it fails in **one** (per-passage orders correctly).
The reading is now *generated from the computed table*, and a test requires the
two to agree. Writing a conclusion by hand next to a computed figure is how they
drift. A separate check confirms the METHODS §7g table matches `city_schemes.csv`
row by row.

## 4. Things I did not do, and why

*(The four items previously listed here were closed in the second pass — see
§3a. What follows is what remains.)*

- **The charge level is fixed in the joint sweep**, so it answers *when*, not
  when and how much jointly.
- **Bargaining is one-shot.** Neither side builds a reputation, a failed vote
  cannot be re-proposed, and the 2027 date is a hard boundary rather than a
  lever. All three understate the value of moving early.
- **The two gates are assumed independent.** In practice a popular scheme is
  easier to authorise, which understates the value of waiting for support.
- **The transfer date is treated as certain.** It has slipped before.
- **§11A-1 and A-2 still dominate everything here.** A real screenline count
  and probe travel times, and a TTS extract. Both are data requests, not coding
  tasks, and every figure above is downstream of them.

## 5. New assumptions introduced — all sweepable, none measured

| Parameter | Default | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `rejoin_fraction` | 0.55 | Unchanged in value; now reachable by robustness |
| `delivered_alternative_bonus` | 0.45 | Support gained from a *visible* alternative. The sequencing result rests entirely on this |
| `enactment_threshold` / `steepness` | 0.50 / 14 | Political gate; logistic, not a step, so the ranking is not a cliff artefact |
| `transit_delivery_lag_years` | 2 | Deliberately optimistic — a longer lag only strengthens transit-first |
| `political_cost_per_charged_trip` | 0.90 | Level is arbitrary; the result rests on the *ratio* between players, which is derived from geography |
| `province_marginal_weight` | 1.60 | Set to 1.0 to see what the 905-seat asymmetry was doing |
| `bargaining_power_city` | 0.50 | Not observable — the whole curve is reported |

`delivered_alternative` defaults to **0**, so nothing outside the phased game
moves. A test sets the coefficient to 25 to prove the term cannot reach a
single-shot run.
