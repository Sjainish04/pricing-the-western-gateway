## 1. What this is

A bounded-corridor **Stackelberg game** for a peak-period congestion charge on the
Gardiner Expressway's western gateway (Highway 427 → Humber River), built from
`UTTAN_Game_Theory_Simulations_Next_Steps.md` and
`UTTAN_Revised_Proposal_Etobicoke_Gardiner.md`, then deepened using six mentor
PDFs from Dr Imran.

It answers the four questions the proposal poses — **where** to price, **what** the
price should be, **who** to protect, **what** to do with the money — and tests the
charge against a serious non-priced alternative rather than assuming pricing wins.

~5,700 lines of Python, ~6,050 lines of frontend, 10 data files, 52 tests.

## 4. The model

**Network.** Five links, split at the Humber:

```
gardiner_west    Hwy 427 → Humber        ← the charged segment, 8.2 km
gardiner_east    Humber → downtown       ← shared by everybody, 10.3 km
lakeshore_west   the City-road bypass of the charged segment
lakeshore_east   Lake Shore into downtown
queensway_west   the secondary bypass
```

The split is the single most consequential structural decision. The charged
segment is 8.2 km of an 18.5 km trip, so the cheapest evasion is exiting before
the gantry and rejoining past it. A single-link corridor reported +0.5% diversion
at \$4 — implausible, and the first thing a judge would attack. Split, it reports
**+11.6% on The Queensway**, which breaches the diversion cap and drives the
recommendation toward full screenline coverage.

**Eight follower actions:** drive Gardiner, bypass via Lake Shore, bypass via
Queensway, carpool (HOV2+), retime to shoulder, Lakeshore West GO, TTC, don't
travel. **Nested logit**, not plain MNL — MNL's IIA would make a priced-off driver
as likely to board a train as to divert, understating the study's central risk.

**70 segments:** 7 sub-areas × 5 income quintiles × fixed/flexible schedule.
Transit access is a property of the sub-area. **Mississauga / west GTA carries 40%
of demand** — a large share of those charged are not Toronto residents.

**Link performance:** `t(v) = t_free + d_signal + t_free·α·(v/c)^β`, β fixed at
4.5, α fitted.

### Calibration and validation

| Link | Role | α | v/c | Error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gardiner_west | calibrated | 0.906 | 0.990 | +0.00% |
| gardiner_east | calibrated | 1.756 | 0.936 | +0.00% |
| lakeshore_west | calibrated | 1.173 | 0.871 | +0.00% |
| **lakeshore_east** | **held out** | 1.173 | 0.632 | **−6.32%** |
| **queensway_west** | **held out** | 1.173 | 0.533 | **−2.99%** |

Two links are held out and inherit the fitted arterial α — their error is a
genuine out-of-sample check. Mode-share error: **0.037 pp**.

## 5. Game theory

**Three levels: leader → travellers → voters.**

| Benchmark | Value |
|---|---|
| Pigouvian first-best, charged segment | **\$9.73** |
| Pigouvian, full Gardiner corridor | \$28.07 |
| Welfare-maximising uniform screenline charge | \$15.50 |
| Price of anarchy | **1.140** |
| Annual welfare lost to selfish choice | **\$55.9M** |

The untolled downstream section carries a larger externality (\$18/vehicle) than
the charged one — and a gateway charge cannot touch it. That gap is the clearest
statement of why the answer is not "charge the Pigouvian toll".

**Constrained leader search:** 193 designs over price × window × coverage ×
credit, subject to diversion cap, equity floor, minimum congestion improvement,
transit capacity, **and acceptability**. 92 feasible.

**Acceptability (3rd level, from Eliasson + case studies).** Support is modelled
over the **consumer** perspective (own net position) and the **citizen**
perspective (whether pricing is seen as a fair allocation mechanism at all, which
rises with income — making pricing partly an elite project regardless of how the
money is spent), plus the experience shift. Reproduces the Stockholm anchors
(≈38% before → ≈61% after). Tested *after* operation, since requiring majority
support up front would rule out every charge ever built.

**Ride-hailing** is a second follower choosing pass-through, which comes out
interior — which is why charging the platform is not equivalent to charging the
rider (London removed its exemption; NYC charges \$2.75 solo / \$0.75 shared).

## 6. Equity — the deepened layer

Built from the mentor PDFs. **The model implements their methods, it does not
just cite them.**

**Eliasson's four welfare components.** Tolls paid is only the first. Components
1–3 are computed exactly as the change in the nested-logit **logsum** (the
compensating variation), verified against an analytic case: raising every option
by \$5 costs exactly \$5 of surplus.

**The two numbers that disagree** — under a flat \$10 screenline charge:

| | Q1 (\$24k) | Q5 (\$215k) |
|---|---|---|
| Toll paid per trip | \$2.43 | \$5.48 |
| Welfare loss, **% of income** | **−1.82%** | **−0.11%** |

Q5 pays more than twice the dollars; Q1 loses **17× more of its income**. Both are
true. Reporting only the first is how equity analyses mislead.

**Suits benchmarks** (real schemes, from Eliasson Table 2): Stockholm −0.09,
Helsinki −0.09, Gothenburg −0.13 (−0.06 without the company-car exemption), Lyon
−0.16. US sales tax −0.11, Texas fuel tax −0.25. **This corridor's flat design is
more regressive than every operating charge.**

**Also implemented:** burden concentration above income thresholds (averages hide
the harmed subgroup); FHWA's income/geographic/modal equity as three distinct
types; toll-account access barrier (10–20% cannot hold an account and pay through
a costlier channel); Shaheen's **STEPS** framework with Physiological and Social
reported as *not modelled*, with reasons, rather than scored as passes;
Greenlining/SF **means-tested pricing** (Q1 exempt, Q2 half) — the only design
that reverses the burden ordering.

**Revenue recycling is capped** at `recycling_capture_share = 0.5`. Crediting the
whole of net revenue back to the 40,465 corridor travellers made every
revenue-positive scheme a Pareto improvement on paper — an artefact, not a
finding. Results are reported before *and* after recycling. **Before recycling, a
flat \$10 charge leaves 0% of travellers better off.**

## 8. Headline findings

1. **Gateway choice is robust.** Etobicoke–Gardiner wins **95.8%** of 4,000
   alternative criterion weightings; TOPSIS agrees. It also scores *worst of five*
   on governance, which the screening states rather than buries.
2. **A freeway-only charge leaks badly.** \$4 pushes +11.6% onto The Queensway,
   breaching the 10% cap. Full screenline coverage turns diversion negative.
3. **Pricing is not obviously better than good operations.** The no-charge
   operational package removes **33.9%** of delay against **35.5%** for a \$5
   screenline charge. Pricing wins by ~2 points at a political cost operations
   don't carry. (The 20.8pp "advantage" figure in the comparison is against the
   strongest *priced* scenario, which includes a parking surcharge.)
4. **A single-corridor pilot is financially marginal.** Fixed collection costs
   don't scale down; at modest charges the scheme can spend more collecting than
   it raises, especially once credits are paid.
5. **Equity design is what makes a scheme passable**, not a cost imposed on it.
   Only the equity-first (50%) and targeted (54%) packages would win a vote today.
6. **Branch B is the most regressive option of all.** Charging only City arterials
   — the fallback if provincial authorisation never arrives — prices the road
   lower-income Lake Shore residents use while leaving the expressway free.

### Current recommendation

`/api/recommendation` → **\$10, full western screenline, 06:30–09:30, income +
transit-poor credits**: −7.6% peak vehicles, **39.4%** delay removed, **\$5.55M**
net revenue, equity 0.558, support 38% → 61%, **63% of travellers better off**.
Cost of the constraints: 0.196 welfare points against the unconstrained optimum.

## 9. Bugs found and fixed — institutional knowledge

Every one of these produced a **plausible number, not a crash**. This is the
failure mode to watch for in this codebase.

**Model:**
1. **Suits index sign inverted** (`2L−1` instead of `1−2L`) — reported a
   regressive charge as progressive. Now pinned by a test against four known cases.
2. **Toll divided by occupancy only for carpools** — 1.15-occupancy arterial
   routes over-collected 15%. Caught because "average charge collected" read
   \$5.22 on a \$5 toll.
3. **Transit credits netted off the burden** — a large enough subsidy could show a
   charge as costless. Rebates and transit credits are now separated.
4. **Parking surcharge in neither the equity nor revenue account** — the model was
   recommending a parking levy as free congestion relief.
5. **Untolled baseline charged the operating cost** of a scheme never built.
6. **100% of net revenue credited to corridor travellers** — made every
   revenue-positive scheme a Pareto improvement.

**Methodology:**
7. **Sensitivity was re-fitting the choice constants per draw** — circular; the
   constants absorb the parameter and the analysis reports near-zero sensitivity.
   Now held fixed at the central config. Also took the tornado 65s → 1.8s.

**Performance:**
8. `network.route_composition()` re-parsed a DataFrame with `iterrows()` on every
   solver iteration (460 calls/run). Caching took a run **1160ms → 9ms**.
   *Profile before assuming numpy is the bottleneck here — it never is; pandas row
   iteration is.*

**Map / frontend:**
9. **MapLibre v6 has no default export** — `import maplibregl from 'maplibre-gl'`
   silently yields `undefined`.
10. **An animated `line-dasharray` on a 90ms interval froze the renderer** — every
    dasharray change rebuilds MapLibre's line-atlas texture. Removed.
11. **Gantry portals floated up to 578m off the highway** — gantry points and road
    centrelines were digitised separately. Now snapped to the centreline.
12. **Leaflet + React StrictMode** — an async `import('leaflet')` can resolve
    against a container React already detached. Needs three guards.
13. **`map.on('error')` filtering on `status >= 400`** swallowed WebGL and style
    errors — the informative ones.
14. **Highlighted table rows used the sequential data ramp as UI chrome**, so dark
    mode couldn't restyle them without repainting the heatmap's encoded values.

### The environment thing that cost the most time

**WebGL maps do not render in the Claude Code preview pane and never will.** The
pane keeps `document.visibilityState` permanently `"hidden"`, so
`requestAnimationFrame` never fires; MapLibre drives its whole pipeline off rAF,
so it constructs cleanly, attaches controls, resolves the style, shows its
attribution bar — and then sits forever with a blank canvas. Calling `_render()`
manually **freezes the renderer and times out CDP**. Leaflet works fine there
(DOM `<img>` tiles + SVG).

Hence: probe `webgl && rAF` up front (`lib/capabilities.js`), fall back to
Leaflet, **and** accept a runtime failure report from the 3D map — a pre-flight
probe alone is not enough, and an explicit user override must not outrank a
failure report.

**Verify WebGL map geometry numerically in Node** (ring closure, measured widths
in metres). Visual confirmation is impossible in this environment.

**PDFs:** `poppler` is not installed, so the `Read` tool fails on PDFs. Use
`pypdf` to extract text to the scratchpad first.

## 10. Known limitations — read before quoting any figure

Listed in full on the **Data & provenance** page. The ones that matter most:

- **Screenline demand is derived, not counted.** 120,000 two-way weekday
  interpolated between the published 140k system average and the 164k downtown
  count.
- **Observed travel times are estimates.** α is fitted to them, so an error here
  propagates into every delay result.
- **The OD pattern is synthetic** — Census-derived, not a TTS extract.
- **The rejoin fraction (55%) drives the diversion result** and has no local
  observation behind it.
- **Collection costs are assumed and change the sign of the financial answer.**
- **Acceptability coefficients are a calibrated stance, not estimates.** Eliasson
  fits an ordered logit to survey data this project does not have; the
  coefficients are set to reproduce the ⅓-before / ⅔-after anchors.
- **Emissions are a proxy, not an inventory.**
- **Ride-hail and freight are not separately calibrated** beyond the pass-through
  model.
- **No microsimulation** — no individual vehicles, queues, signals or incidents.
- **Physiological and Social equity (STEPS) are not modelled at all**, and say so.

## 11. Next steps

### A. Before the competition submission (highest value, low effort)

1. **Get one real screenline count and probe travel times** from City of Toronto
   Transportation Services. These are the two most consequential ESTIMATED inputs
   and both are measurable. Everything else is downstream of them.
2. **Replace the synthetic OD with a TTS extract** (DMG, U of T). The segmentation
   is representative but not observed; a real extract would let the equity results
   carry proper weight.
3. **Sanity-check the acceptability coefficients with a small survey** — even
   n≈150 of corridor commuters would move this from a calibrated stance to an
   estimate, and it is the newest and least grounded part of the model.
4. **Interview the two people the proposal commits to.** UTTRI on the behavioural
   parameters and the rejoin fraction; Metrolinx on Lakeshore West peak capacity,
   which currently sets the transit-capacity constraint.

### B. Modelling depth (medium effort, high defensibility)

5. **Validate the rejoin fraction against the Gardiner Section 3 construction
   period.** That closure is a natural experiment for exactly the gantry-hopping
   behaviour the model assumes at 55%. This is the single best available test of
   the structural decision the whole diversion result rests on.
6. **Add the phased/sequential game.** Stockholm expanded transit *first*; NYC's
   plan is explicitly phased (transit → ride-hail surcharge → cordon). Currently
   the leader moves once. A two-stage game where stage 1 is transit investment and
   acceptability is a state variable would let the model answer *when* to charge,
   not just *how much* — and the case studies say sequencing decides whether a
   scheme survives.
7. **Model the City ↔ Province bargaining problem.** The Gardiner transfers in
   fall 2027 and a toll needs provincial authorisation. A Nash bargaining solution
   over the revenue split and the authorisation decision would turn the governance
   branches from two scenarios into an actual game.
8. **Freight and commercial vehicles as a separate class.** The instrument exists
   (`freight_multiplier`) but is uncalibrated. Off-peak delivery incentives are in
   the proposal's scenario list and currently unmodelled.

### C. Analysis gaps

9. **Geographic equity deserves its own page.** FHWA's NYC analysis found New
   Jersey residents paying 45% of tolls while making up 24% of drivers. This model
   has the same structure — 40% of demand is Mississauga / west GTA — and the
   result is computed but under-surfaced.
10. **Add a distributional map layer.** Burden by sub-area is computed; showing it
    geographically would make the spatial-equity argument far harder to wave away.
11. **Revenue recycling needs a benefit-cost treatment.** Currently spending is
    allocated but its benefit is credited at face value with a capture cap. A
    proper BCR per programme would make the recycling case much stronger.

### D. Hygiene

12. **Move the launch configs into the project** once the preview tooling allows a
    `cwd` outside the primary root, and drop the LIMS-project dependency.
13. **The test suite takes ~35–50s**, dominated by full Stackelberg searches. Mark
    the slow ones so `pytest -m "not slow"` gives a fast inner loop.
14. **Nothing is in version control.** `git init` and commit before the submission
    deadline.
